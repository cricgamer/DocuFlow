Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about instant peer-to-peer screen sharing.

ScreenEcho leverages native browser WebRTC (Web Real-Time Communication). Unlike traditional tools like Zoom or Teams that route your video through heavy corporate cloud servers, ScreenEcho connects the broadcaster directly to the receiver's browser. Because there are no middleman video servers buffering or processing your feed, there are zero server bandwidth bills — allowing ScreenEcho to be 100% free, fast, and accessible without signing up or creating an account.
Yes. Every WebRTC peer-to-peer media stream is natively encrypted end-to-end using DTLS (Datagram Transport Layer Security) and SRTP (Secure Real-time Transport Protocol). No third party, server, or ISP can eavesdrop or decrypt the video frames in transit. Furthermore, ScreenEcho does not record, store, or cache any screen data.
To connect devices across routers, firewalls, and cellular networks, ScreenEcho uses free public Google STUN servers (stun.l.google.com:19302). STUN discovers each device's public IP address and NAT port mappings so the two browsers can handshake and establish a direct P2P tunnel using the 4-digit session code as the rendezvous key.
Yes! When you click "Start Sharing", your browser will display its native screen capture dialog. Simply toggle the "Share tab audio" or "Share system audio" checkbox in Chrome, Edge, or Firefox. The live high-fidelity audio stream will be beamed alongside the video directly to the receiver.
Absolutely. While mobile operating systems restrict broad screen broadcasting from mobile browsers, mobile devices are fantastic receivers. Simply scan the presenter's QR code with your mobile camera or open the share link in Safari or Chrome to watch in full-screen real-time HD with audio.
